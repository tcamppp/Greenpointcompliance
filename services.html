Engagements

Scoped work, not billable hours

Every engagement has a stated scope, a stated deliverable, and a price agreed before work starts. The starting figures below are real — what moves them is the number of tools in scope and the state of your data, not how long the work takes.

Exposure review — $1,500

One week. $1,500, credited in full against a subsequent bias audit. The right starting point when you don't yet know whether Local Law 144 applies to you.

  • Inventory of every system touching hiring, screening, ranking, assessment or promotion
  • Written AEDT determination for each, applying the DCWP final rules rather than a vendor's marketing claim
  • Identification of where in your stack exposure sits — usually the applicant tracking system or an assessment provider
  • A scoping memo you can hand directly to employment counsel

If the answer is that nothing you use meets the definition, you get that in writing and the engagement ends there. That is a legitimate outcome and roughly a third of reviews reach it.

Independent bias audit — from $9,000

Two to three weeks. Annual renewal. One tool on clean historical data sits at the starting figure. Multiple tools, multiple configurations, or reconstructing demographic data you never collected move it up.

  • Selection rate and scoring rate analysis by sex, race and ethnicity, and intersectional categories
  • Impact ratio calculation against the highest-selected group, with four-fifths guideline assessment
  • Treatment of unknown-category individuals, and disclosure language where historical data is insufficient
  • Drafted public summary meeting the 6 RCNY § 5-301 content requirements
  • Review of your candidate notice and opt-out process for practical availability
  • Retained workpapers supporting every figure published

Independence is the point of the engagement. Greenpoint does not maintain relationships with AEDT vendors, does not operate an implementation practice, and has no financial interest in what the numbers say.

AI governance baseline — from $15,000

45 days. For organizations whose AI exposure runs past hiring.

  • Enterprise AI system inventory and use-case classification
  • Risk tiering mapped to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework
  • Control design, ownership assignment, and evidence retention practices
  • Gap assessment against EU AI Act deployer obligations where you sell into the EU
  • Board- or committee-ready summary of the program and its residual risks

What actually drives the number. Not hours. Three things: how many AEDTs are in scope, whether you have usable historical data or need test data substituted, and how many demographic categories produce group sizes large enough to analyse. A quote follows the scoping call, in writing, before you commit to anything.

Ongoing advisory. Some clients keep a standing monthly arrangement for regulatory monitoring, new-tool reviews, and annual audit renewal. That runs $2,000–$4,000 a month depending on scope, and it is only worth it once you have more than one tool in play.

How engagements start

  1. Scoping call, 20 minutes, free. Usually enough to determine whether you're covered.
  2. Written proposal with fixed scope, price, and dates. No retainer required to receive it.
  3. Kickoff with a data request list. Data quality drives the timeline more than anything else.
  4. Delivery of workpapers, published summary language, and a walkthrough with whoever owns the decision.
