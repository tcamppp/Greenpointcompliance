Scoped work, not billable hours

Every engagement has a stated scope, a stated deliverable, and a price agreed before work starts. The starting figures below are real — what moves them is the number of tools in scope and the state of your data.

Exposure review — from $3,500

The right starting point when you don't yet know whether Local Law 144 applies to you.

  • Inventory of every system touching hiring, screening, ranking, assessment or promotion
  • Written AEDT determination for each, applying the DCWP final rules rather than a vendor's marketing claim
  • Identification of where in your stack exposure sits — usually the applicant tracking system or an assessment provider
  • A scoping memo you can hand directly to employment counsel

If the answer is that nothing you use meets the definition, you get that in writing and the engagement ends there. That is a legitimate outcome and roughly a third of reviews reach it.

Independent bias audit — from $9,000

Annual renewal. The audit the law actually requires, conducted by an auditor with no financial interest in the outcome.

  • Impact ratio analysis by sex, by race and ethnicity, and by intersectional category
  • Selection and scoring rate calculations against the four-fifths rule
  • Treatment of categories where group sizes are too small to analyse, documented rather than quietly dropped
  • The published summary drafted in the form the rules require
  • Candidate notice language reviewed against your careers flow
  • Workpapers retained, so the analysis survives a challenge

One tool on clean historical data sits at the starting figure. Multiple tools, multiple configurations, or reconstructing demographic data you never collected move it up.

NIST AI RMF governance baseline — from $15,000

For organizations whose exposure runs past hiring, or who need a defensible governance story for a customer, an insurer, or a board.

  • Inventory of AI and automated decision systems across the business
  • Risk classification under the NIST AI Risk Management Framework
  • Control design proportionate to actual risk, not a copied maturity model
  • Evidence practices that make the program auditable later
  • EU AI Act deployer gap assessment where the obligations reach you

One audit substantially serves obligations across NYC, Illinois, Colorado and the EU. The overlap is real and worth scoping deliberately rather than paying for three times.

Why independence matters here

Local Law 144 requires that the auditor hold no financial relationship with the employer or with the tool being audited. Greenpoint does not sell AI tooling, does not implement hiring systems, and does not maintain vendor partnerships. The only product is the audit and the evidence behind it.

A note on scope

Coverage follows the candidate, not the company. If you hire remotely and one applicant lives in the five boroughs, that evaluation is in scope — regardless of where you are headquartered. Most organizations discover their exposure inside an applicant tracking system they assumed was manual.
